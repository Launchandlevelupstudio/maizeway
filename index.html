Projects rarely fail because people stopped working.

They fail because ownership got blurry, the sequence was wrong from the start, or nobody had visibility until a date was already missed. By the time it's obvious, the fix costs three times what it would have.

Too many initiatives, not enough sequence.

Everything is a priority, so capacity gets split until nothing lands.

No single owner.

Work crosses four teams and belongs to none of them. Decisions wait.

Status you can't trust.

The MaizeWay Method

Five stages. Each one produces something your team keeps — and the last one is the point.

  • 01

    Discover

    Objectives, constraints, stakeholders, what's already been tried.

    You get: Engagement brief and success criteria

    Week 1

  • 02

    Diagnose

    Where the work is actually breaking down — process, ownership, sequence, or capacity.

    You get: Findings report with prioritized root causes

    Weeks 1–2

  • 03

    Architect

    Design the operating model that fits your constraints.

    You get: Operating model, RACI, sequenced plan, governance and reporting design

    Weeks 2–4

  • 04

    Execute

    Lead the work through delivery; adjust as reality intervenes.

    You get: Weekly reporting, a managed risk register, decisions driven to close

    Weeks 4–12

  • 05

    Hand Off

    Transfer the structure to your team.

    You get: SOPs, templates, documented governance, a transition session

    Final 2 weeks

    • Most engagements move through all five stages in 30 to 90 days. The scope of an engagement is the initiative, not the calendar.

    How an engagement works

    Fractional program leadership with a clear arc — from the first conversation through a clean handoff your team can run without me.

    1. 01Before kickoff

      Intro call

      A 30-minute conversation about the initiative, constraints, and whether MaizeWay is the right fit.

    2. 02Days 1–7

      Week 1

      Discover and diagnose: map stakeholders, surface what's already been tried, and name the real failure points.

    3. 03Design phase

      Weeks 2–4

      Architect the operating model — ownership, sequence, governance, and the reporting cadence leadership will trust.

    4. 04Delivery

      Weeks 4–12

      Execute: lead delivery, drive decisions to close, and keep risk and status visible week by week.

    5. 05Final weeks

      Handoff

      Transfer the structure to your team — SOPs, templates, governance, and a clean transition session.
